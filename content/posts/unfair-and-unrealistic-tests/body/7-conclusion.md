The best education is about developing students' minds, and testing should verify the extent to which that has been achieved at a particular point in time.<sup>[3](#footnote-3)</sup> Moreover, testing can be divided into verification while learning is happening (i.e., during education) and after the fact (e.g., in technical interviews), which serve to gain a broader assessment of a person's attributes and attitude.

While it would be ideal to educate and test via large scale projects, completed in realistic working conditions, it is unfortunately misguided to do so now. On one hand, this is because AI assistance can facilitate the completion of those projects at the expense of student's understanding. On the other hand, students need controlled, distraction-free environments to hone in on developing specific skills.

Hence, instead of taking our feet off the brakes and risk letting the value of education deteriorate, we need to tune our existing brakes for the new surfaces we're driving on.

Ultimately, pen-and-paper or offline computing should be the predominant medium, and subjects emphasising fundamentals skills should predominate courses. Then, insofar as projects should not be completed offline or in-person: human-centric and improvisational add-ons should be an increasingly large part of those projects (e.g., oral examination).

Then, in the post-education context (e.g., technical interviews), testing should capture signals about whether a candidates are deeply interested and curious, have actually gotten their hands dirty, and are not shy about reading deeply. Importantly, to achieve that, subtle and strange questions often need to be asked, and most importantly, we need to be ok with that process making predictability and perfection hard to come by.
