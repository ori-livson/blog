Similarly, HTMX's creator and Montana State University instructor Carson Gross said in an [interview](https://youtu.be/VedijY4W5bU?t=2790) that because his students now typically score A's in assignments due to AI, he is increasing the weighting of his pen-and-paper quizzes to 50% of their total grade. Previously, ~70% of their total grade came from a large coding assignment.

However, these quizzes are not merely a cynical mechanism for balancing grades, his aim is to emphasise code annotation, review and analysis, which represent deeply valuable fundamentals (more on this later).

So, before getting into other strategies to testing smarter without overreliance on assignments and take-home exams, I want to discuss what it means to educate in fundamentals, and why it often needs to be done in contrast to our obsession with things being "realistic".
