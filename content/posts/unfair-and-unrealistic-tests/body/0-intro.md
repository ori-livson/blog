The following are understandable criticisms of schooling and testing (and not just because I have held them all at some point).

1. Timed exams are unfair because the conditions are unrealistic.
2. Closed-book exams are especially unrealistic.
3. Curricula should emphasise more "real world content".

These criticisms are commonly levied against high schools, universities, technical interviews for jobs, and many other domains.

However, the purpose of this post is to explore whether in the current age of AI disruption in education and testing: will indulging these criticisms make things worse? In other words: is the trend of making everything assignment / project-based and in particular AI-enabled misguided

In the educational context I will argue that the above is so because: **core knowledge ought to be learned properly; capacity for independence, patience, careful reading and memorisation matters -- but sometimes those attributes require unrealistic settings to be developed in.**

Moreover, sleepwalking into letting students complete all of their assessments with minimal understanding of the content via AI overdependence, will simply deteriorate what a degree signal about a graduate's ability.

In the context of technical interviews, a subtly related argument is that **we should not shy away from trivia.** Trivia being peripheral, perhaps strange or edge-case questions, whose answers tend to be learned through experience, interest, getting one's hands dirty, deep rather than surface level reading, etc. In other words, good testing in this context should require some serendipity but not too much for good candidates.

Of course there are a lot of wrong ways to do old-fashioned education and testing, but I hope to demonstrate there is actually a lot to be gained by taking a step back and figuring out how to educate and test effectively, rather than clumsily indulging AI inevitabilism.
