The following are reasonable criticisms of education and testing (not just because I have held them all at some point).

1. Exams are unfair because the conditions are unrealistic.
2. Closed-book exams are especially unrealistic and unfair.
3. Curricula are unrealistic and unfair when they do not emphasise "real world content".

These criticisms are commonly levied against high schools, universities, technical interviews for jobs, and in many other domains.

However, the purpose of this post is to argue that in the current age of AI disruption in education and testing, mindlessly indulging these criticisms will just make things worse. In other words, it is a mistake to make everything assignment / project-based, while encouraging students to eagerly use AI.

In the education context: **core knowledge ought to be learned properly; capacity for independence, patience, careful reading and memorisation matters; and mental muscles need to be exercised** -- but sometimes **these attributes require unrealistic settings to be developed in.**

Moreover, if we sleepwalk into letting students complete all their assessments with minimal understanding of the content via AI overdependence, the signal of what a degree says about a student's ability will simply deteriorate.

In technical interviewing, the subtly related remedy is that **we should not shy away from trivia**. Trivia meaning peripheral, perhaps strange or edge-case questions whose answers tend to be known through experience, interest, getting one's hands dirty, deep reading rather than surface level reading, etc. In other words, good testing in this context should require some serendipity but very little for candidates with good attributes and experience.

Of course there are a lot of wrong ways to do old-fashioned education and testing, but I hope to show there is actually a lot to be gained by taking a step back figuring out how to educate and test effectively, rather than clumsily indulging AI inevitabilism.

Furthermore, I will argue that without an "exercised mind", not only does further learning continue to become more difficult, but we may inadvertently produce a generation largely unable to ask anything interesting of AI. Indeed, having had the oppurtunity of good educations, we should not pull the ladder up from beneath us.
