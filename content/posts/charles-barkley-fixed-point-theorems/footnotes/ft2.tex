To axiomatise arithmetic, one has to distinguish between numbers and \textbf{numerals} (i.e., the symbolic representation of a number), e.g., using a zero-numeral $)$ and a successor operation $S$, we have the numerals for $1,2,3$ given by $S0$, $SS0$, $SSS0$, respectively.

As such, for formulae $f \in F$, ${}^\ulcorner\ f {}^\urcorner$ is often reserved to denote the \textbf{numeral} of the Gödel number of $f$. Although, for a predicate $A(x)$, one can abuse notation and have ${}^\ulcorner\ f {}^\urcorner$ as a number in isolation, and $A({}^\ulcorner\ f {}^\urcorner)$ as $A(x)$ with the numeral for ${}^\ulcorner\ f {}^\urcorner$ substituted in the place of $x$.
